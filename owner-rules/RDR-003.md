# Roger's Durable Rule — RDR-003
## Educational visual accuracy, realism and vocabulary-learning priority

**Owner directive:** 2026-11-24. **Status:** ACTIVE · ADDITIVE · PERMANENT.
**Scope:** Every present and future Roger-owned builder and educational application, website, textbook chapter, lesson, language, subject, exercise, game, learning resource and visual asset. Applies to all builders whenever their work uses educational imagery; ControlCenter coordinates dissemination and evidence, not sibling edits. RDR-001, RDR-002 and all other existing rules remain unchanged.

### Binding owner requirement
Pictures are a teaching priority, not decoration. Every builder must audit imagery for realism, accuracy and actual usefulness in helping students learn the intended vocabulary or concept. This requirement applies to the entire existing application and to every new chapter, textbook, language or subject matter in the future.

### Mandatory acceptance standard
1. Inspect the actual rendered image alongside its intended word, meaning, context, clue and teaching objective. A filename, alt text, prompt, image count or successful load alone is not proof of educational quality.
2. Prefer clear, recognizable, natural and realistic photographs or photo-quality illustrations when teaching tangible objects, people, actions, places or physical phenomena. Use accurate diagrams or clear visual metaphors when these genuinely teach abstract concepts better. Do not impose photorealism on diagrams where clarity is superior.
3. Reject distorted anatomy, impossible poses, malformed objects, misleading scale or context, irrelevant stock imagery, generic placeholders, misleading visual associations and unclear target subjects. An image for branches must visibly show actual tree branches; an action scene must show a believable person performing the action with realistic limbs and posture.
4. Match the precise sense of the word in context and the learner's level. Check culturally appropriate, age-appropriate, respectful imagery, factual accuracy, and translation alignment. Do not use recognizable pupil images or collect student data without existing separate authorization and safeguards.
5. Verify every new or changed educational image at actual display sizes, including the intended projector or mobile layout where applicable. Check sharpness, crop, contrast, legibility, clear focal subject, hidden-answer behavior and offline availability where required. Preserve effective existing images and replace weak ones with demonstrably better assets rather than making cosmetic changes.
6. Audit the complete existing image inventory, not merely recently changed pictures. Record coverage, asset-to-vocabulary mappings, reviewed examples, defects, replacements, remaining gaps and provenance/licensing. Reused imagery is acceptable only if each mapping genuinely teaches its target. Distinguish a loaded asset from a visually approved one. Hold or clearly disclose incomplete visual qualification; never claim a comprehensive pass from sampling alone.
7. Carry this exact rule into each relevant next-version Builder, prebuild/postbuild visual quality gate and release evidence. Prioritize corrections of weak educational images before introducing new features. Preserve predecessor assets and rollback; no silent deletion, forced network dependency or cross-project edits. Independent recipient acknowledgment and real device review remain separate from self-reported tests.

**Required evidence:** VISUAL_AUDIT_COVERAGE, WORD_IMAGE_ALIGNMENT, REALISM_AND_ANATOMY, LEARNER_CLARITY, AGE_AND_CULTURAL_SUITABILITY, PROVENANCE, DISPLAY_AND_OFFLINE_CHECK, and documented unresolved defects. No dilution of earlier rules. ControlCenter should route this additive rule to all relevant builders and track independent adoption separately; posting alone is not acknowledgment.
